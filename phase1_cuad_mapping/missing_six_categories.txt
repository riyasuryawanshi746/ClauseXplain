CLAUSEXPLAIN -- SIX TARGET CATEGORIES WITH ZERO CUAD-DERIVED TRAINING SIGNAL
=============================================================================

IMPORTANT CORRECTION BEFORE READING FURTHER:

The original project framing assumed the sixth zero-coverage category was
"Dispute Resolution." Verified directly against the final 41->18 mapping,
this is NOT correct. Dispute Resolution receives 173 CUAD-derived spans
(via the CUAD category "Covenant Not To Sue"), so it has narrow but
non-zero coverage -- it is inadequately covered, not absent.

The sixth category that is actually and fully absent (zero spans, verified
directly against clauses_mapped.jsonl) is PAYMENT. This is a direct
consequence of the decision to scope "Payment" strictly to
invoicing/payment-timing/interest-on-late-payment provisions and route all
commercial/deal-structure provisions (Minimum Commitment, Revenue Sharing,
etc.) to the new "Commercial Terms" category instead. None of CUAD's 41
categories concern payment timing or invoicing at all, so once
"Commercial Terms" absorbed the closest adjacent CUAD categories, Payment
was left with nothing.

The corrected list of six categories with ZERO CUAD-derived spans is:
  1. Indemnification
  2. Confidentiality
  3. Privacy / Data Protection
  4. Modification
  5. Force Majeure
  6. Payment

Dispute Resolution is a separate, SEVENTH category worth flagging in the
paper as "narrowly/inadequately covered" (173 spans, but only from one
CUAD category that covers a small slice of what "dispute resolution"
usually means -- see its own entry below for detail).

=============================================================================

1. INDEMNIFICATION
-------------------
Why CUAD does not adequately cover it:
  None of CUAD's 41 categories concern indemnification. CUAD's annotation
  scheme was built around what a corporate/M&A lawyer reviews in SEC-filed
  commercial agreements; indemnification, while extremely common in real
  contracts, was not isolated as one of the 41 categories CUAD's authors
  chose to annotate.

Consequence for ClauseXplain:
  The neural component receives zero training signal for this category
  from CUAD. Any indemnification-risk detection must come entirely from
  the symbolic rule layer or from a supplementary annotated dataset.

Indian-law relevance:
  Indemnity clauses are governed generally by contract principles under
  the Indian Contract Act, 1872 (Sections 124-125 define indemnity and a
  right to indemnity). Uncapped or unusually broad indemnity obligations
  are a commonly cited risk for freelancers and SMEs. (Verify current
  case law before final publication -- this note states the general
  statutory basis, not a specific risk threshold.)

Current ClauseXplain handling:
  Symbolic rule layer only (existing rule ICA_007 in the prototype system
  targets indemnity + uncapped-liability co-occurrence).

Future dataset requirement:
  A supplementary corpus of Indian service/vendor/freelance agreements
  with indemnification clauses manually identified and reviewed, or
  LLM-assisted weak labeling validated against a human-annotated subset.

Research-paper wording (Limitations / Threats to Validity):
  "CUAD does not annotate indemnification as a distinct category, as its
  scheme reflects the review priorities of SEC-filed M&A agreements rather
  than the concerns of freelance or SME service contracts. Consequently,
  the neural component of ClauseXplain receives no CUAD-derived training
  signal for indemnification risk; detection currently relies entirely on
  the symbolic rule layer, and closing this gap is identified as a primary
  direction for future dataset expansion."


2. CONFIDENTIALITY
-------------------
Why CUAD does not adequately cover it:
  CUAD's source contracts are commercial/M&A agreements, not standalone
  NDAs; general confidentiality obligations were not tagged as one of the
  41 categories despite being common in real-world contracts.

Consequence for ClauseXplain:
  Zero neural training signal from CUAD for this category.

Indian-law relevance:
  Confidentiality obligations are enforced as ordinary contractual terms
  under the Indian Contract Act, 1872; there is no dedicated confidentiality
  statute analogous to trade-secret statutes in some other jurisdictions.
  (This is a general statement of contract-law treatment, not a claim about
  any specific enforcement outcome.)

Current ClauseXplain handling:
  Not currently covered by either the neural or symbolic layer of the
  prototype system.

Future dataset requirement:
  Same as Indemnification -- supplementary annotation of confidentiality
  clauses from real or synthetic Indian contracts.

Research-paper wording:
  "Confidentiality obligations, though common in real-world commercial
  contracts, are not annotated as a distinct category in CUAD. This
  category currently has no representation in either the neural or
  symbolic components of ClauseXplain and is identified as an open gap."


3. PRIVACY / DATA PROTECTION
-----------------------------
Why CUAD does not adequately cover it:
  CUAD (published 2021, sourced from SEC filings predating comprehensive
  personal-data-protection legislation in many jurisdictions) does not tag
  any category related to processing of personal or sensitive data.

Consequence for ClauseXplain:
  Zero neural training signal. This is the most significant gap for the
  project's stated Indian-law focus, since data-protection risk assessment
  is one of ClauseXplain's central design goals.

Indian-law relevance:
  The Digital Personal Data Protection Act (DPDPA), 2023 is the primary
  relevant statute. (Confirm current provisions and effective-date status
  against the official text before final publication, as implementation
  timelines for DPDPA rules have been subject to change.)

Current ClauseXplain handling:
  Symbolic rule layer only (existing rules DPDPA_001 through DPDPA_004 in
  the prototype system).

Future dataset requirement:
  A dedicated Indian-contract data-protection clause corpus is likely the
  single highest-priority annotation task for this project, given the
  centrality of this category to the stated research contribution.

Research-paper wording:
  "Privacy and data-protection provisions are entirely unrepresented in
  CUAD's annotation scheme, reflecting both its US origin and its 2021
  publication date, prior to the Digital Personal Data Protection Act,
  2023 coming into force in India. This is the most consequential gap for
  a project centered on Indian data-protection risk, and closing it via
  supplementary annotation is treated as the highest-priority direction
  for future work."


4. MODIFICATION
----------------
Why CUAD does not adequately cover it:
  No CUAD category addresses contract amendment/modification mechanics
  (e.g., whether changes require written consent of both parties).

Consequence for ClauseXplain:
  Zero neural training signal.

Indian-law relevance:
  Amendment/modification of a contract is governed by general principles
  of offer, acceptance, and consideration under the Indian Contract Act,
  1872; no single dedicated section addresses "modification clauses" as a
  distinct legal category. (General statement; not a claim of comprehensive
  statutory coverage.)

Current ClauseXplain handling:
  Not currently covered.

Future dataset requirement:
  Supplementary annotation from real or synthetic Indian contracts.

Research-paper wording:
  "No CUAD category addresses contract modification/amendment mechanics.
  This category is currently unsupported by either the neural or symbolic
  components of ClauseXplain."


5. FORCE MAJEURE
------------------
Why CUAD does not adequately cover it:
  No CUAD category addresses force majeure/excused-performance provisions,
  despite their prevalence in real commercial contracts.

Consequence for ClauseXplain:
  Zero neural training signal.

Indian-law relevance:
  Force majeure is addressed both through express contractual clauses and,
  in their absence, through the doctrine of frustration under Section 56
  of the Indian Contract Act, 1872. (General statement of statutory basis;
  the interaction between an express force majeure clause and Section 56
  is a substantive area of case law that should be reviewed separately
  before making specific claims in the paper.)

Current ClauseXplain handling:
  Not currently covered.

Future dataset requirement:
  Supplementary annotation; force majeure clauses became a heavily
  litigated area in India following COVID-19, so recent Indian case law
  may be a useful source of real examples.

Research-paper wording:
  "Force majeure provisions, despite their prevalence in commercial
  contracts and their heightened relevance in India following COVID-19-era
  litigation, are not represented in CUAD's annotation scheme. This
  category is currently unsupported and is identified as a future
  annotation priority."


6. PAYMENT
-----------
Why CUAD does not adequately cover it:
  None of CUAD's 41 categories concern payment timing, invoicing, or
  interest on delayed payment. Categories that superficially seem
  payment-adjacent (Revenue/Profit Sharing, Minimum Commitment, Price
  Restrictions, Volume Restriction) concern deal-structure/commercial
  economics rather than payment mechanics, and were deliberately routed to
  the new "Commercial Terms" category instead of being force-fit into
  Payment. This is a direct, documented consequence of that taxonomy
  decision, not an oversight.

Consequence for ClauseXplain:
  Zero neural training signal for the category that is arguably most
  directly actionable for the project's target users (Indian freelancers
  and SMEs, for whom late payment is a well-documented practical problem).

Indian-law relevance:
  Sections 15-16 of the Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises Development
  (MSMED) Act, 2006 impose a statutory ceiling on buyer credit periods
  (payment due within 45 days of acceptance, absent a shorter agreed date)
  and require compound interest at three times the RBI-notified bank rate
  on delayed payments to micro and small enterprise suppliers. This is a
  directly relevant, verifiable statute for the target user base.

Current ClauseXplain handling:
  Not currently covered by either layer of the prototype system.

Future dataset requirement:
  Supplementary annotation of payment-timing clauses from real Indian
  vendor/freelance contracts; given the MSMED Act's specific, checkable
  45-day threshold, this may be one of the more tractable categories to
  build a symbolic rule for even before neural training data exists
  (a rule checking for the presence/absence of a payment-timing clause and
  flagging terms exceeding 45 days would already be a defensible symbolic
  check that would not require any additional annotated training data).

Research-paper wording:
  "Payment received zero CUAD-derived spans once its scope was strictly
  limited to invoicing/payment-timing provisions, as no CUAD category
  addresses payment mechanics; the categories initially considered adjacent
  (revenue sharing, minimum commitment, etc.) were instead routed to a
  newly introduced Commercial Terms category on the grounds that they
  concern deal-structure economics rather than payment timing. Given the
  direct relevance of Sections 15-16 of the MSMED Act, 2006 to the
  project's target users, this is treated as a near-term priority for
  either supplementary annotation or a rule-based interim solution."


SEVENTH FLAG (not one of the six, but should be disclosed alongside them):

DISPUTE RESOLUTION -- narrow/inadequate coverage, not zero
------------------------------------------------------------
173 spans, but from a single CUAD category ("Covenant Not To Sue") that
addresses only restrictions on a party's ability to bring legal proceedings
-- it says nothing about arbitration clauses, choice of forum/venue, or
mediation, which are the provisions most people mean by "dispute
resolution." This should be disclosed as a coverage gap in the same
"Limitations" section, worded as "narrowly represented" rather than
"absent," to keep the six-category list above accurate.
