PHASE 2 ANNOTATION GUIDELINES -- ClauseXplain
================================================

WHO THIS IS FOR: a human researcher-annotator (not necessarily a lawyer)
scoring CUAD-highlighted spans on the five risk dimensions defined in
risk_scoring_specification.txt. Read that file first.

GENERAL RULES
--------------
1. Score what the clause TEXT actually says, not what you assume about
   the rest of the contract. If a clause references "Section 6.09" and
   you don't have that section, note the missing context rather than
   guessing its content.
2. Never use the word "illegal." Use one or more of the legal_risk_type
   tags instead: potentially unenforceable, potentially non-compliant,
   potentially unfair, ambiguous, commercially unfavorable, jurisdictional
   concern, privacy/data-protection concern, restraint-of-trade concern,
   excessive liability concern, penalty/damages concern.
3. If you cite a specific Indian statutory provision, it must correspond
   to an actual row in indian_legal_rules.csv. If you believe a different
   provision applies that isn't in that file, flag it for the file to be
   updated -- do not invent a citation inline.
4. Set requires_legal_review = True on every record you annotate. This
   project currently has no legal-expert reviewer; your annotation is
   "human_researcher_annotation," not "human_legal_expert_annotation."
5. If you are unsure, use annotation_confidence = "Low" rather than
   guessing at Medium/High to seem more certain than you are.
6. Remember: CUAD spans may be partial excerpts of a larger clause (see
   span_type field). If the highlighted text seems to cut off mid-thought,
   note this -- it affects how confidently you can score ambiguity.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------

UNFAIRNESS_SCORE
-----------------
Low (0.0-0.33): Obligations are roughly symmetric, or asymmetry is
  standard/expected for the clause type (e.g. a warranty clause naturally
  runs one direction, from seller to buyer -- that alone isn't "unfair").

  Example: "Either party may terminate this agreement upon 30 days'
  written notice." -- Both parties have identical rights. Low unfairness.

Medium (0.34-0.66): Asymmetry exists and favors one party more than is
  typical for this clause type, but the disadvantaged party retains some
  meaningful protection or recourse.

  Example: "Client may terminate this agreement upon 30 days' written
  notice. Contractor may terminate only for Client's material,
  uncured breach." -- One-sided termination rights, but Contractor still
  has a real (if narrower) exit path. Medium unfairness.

High (0.67-1.0): Obligations are sharply asymmetric with little or no
  meaningful recourse for the disadvantaged party.

  Example (SYNTHETIC, illustrative only): "Client may terminate this
  agreement at any time, for any reason or no reason, with immediate
  effect and without any payment obligation for work completed but not
  yet invoiced. Contractor may not terminate this agreement under any
  circumstances prior to the completion of all deliverables." -- No
  reciprocal exit right, and the disadvantaged party loses payment for
  completed work. High unfairness.

AMBIGUITY_SCORE
-----------------
Low: Terms are specific and operationally clear (defined dates, defined
  amounts, defined triggering events).

  Example: "Payment is due within 30 days of invoice date." -- Clear,
  operational. Low ambiguity.

Medium: Some terms are undefined or rely on a standard not fully
  specified in the visible text, but the general operation of the clause
  is still discernible.

  Example: "Payment is due within a reasonable time of invoice date." --
  "Reasonable" is undefined. Medium ambiguity.

High: Core operative terms are vague enough that reasonable readers could
  disagree about what obligation actually exists.

  Example (SYNTHETIC): "Contractor shall maintain appropriate
  confidentiality measures as may be necessary from time to time." --
  "Appropriate," "as may be necessary," and "from time to time" are all
  undefined; a reader cannot determine what specific action is required.
  High ambiguity.

NON_COMPLIANCE_SCORE
-----------------------
Only score above 0.0 if you can cite a SPECIFIC row in
indian_legal_rules.csv that the clause's trigger_condition plausibly
matches. If you cannot cite a specific rule, leave this at 0.0 and
consider whether enforceability_score or unfairness_score is the more
appropriate dimension instead.

Low: No plausible match to any rule in indian_legal_rules.csv.
Medium: Plausible match to a rule marked confidence=Low or Medium in
  indian_legal_rules.csv, or the clause's fit to the trigger_condition is
  itself uncertain.
High: Clear match to a rule's trigger_condition, especially one marked
  confidence=High (e.g. ICA_S74_01 for an unmistakable named-penalty
  clause, or MSMED_S16_01 for an explicit late-payment-interest waiver
  where MSME status is independently confirmed).

PRECEDENT_MATCH_SCORE
------------------------
Leave this field null for all Phase 2 annotation. ClauseXplain has no
curated precedent/case-law corpus yet (see risk_scoring_specification.txt,
section 2). Do not attempt to score this dimension until that corpus
exists in a later phase.

ENFORCEABILITY_SCORE
-----------------------
Low: No apparent tension with a legal doctrine that could render any part
  of the clause void or unenforceable.
Medium: The clause resembles a pattern courts have scrutinized (e.g. a
  restraint-of-trade clause) but is narrowly drafted enough that
  enforceability is plausible.
High: The clause resembles a pattern (e.g. an indefinite, unlimited-scope
  non-compete, or an absolute bar on legal proceedings not carved out as
  arbitration) that has a well-documented statutory basis for challenge.

  Example (SYNTHETIC): "Employee shall not, anywhere in the world, for
  a period of 10 years following termination, engage in any business
  activity that competes, directly or indirectly, with Company." --
  Unlimited geographic scope, 10-year duration, broad activity
  definition. High enforceability concern under Section 27.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------

WHAT NOT TO DO
---------------
- Do not assign risk_level directly. It is derived from risk_score, which
  is derived from the five dimension scores. If you find yourself picking
  "High" first and then filling in scores to justify it, stop and score
  the dimensions independently instead.
- Do not leave a dimension blank if you scored it -- use 0.0, not blank,
  if your genuine assessment is "no concern along this dimension."
  Blank/null means "not yet assessed," which is different from "assessed
  as zero."
- Do not annotate a clause you don't have enough context to assess. Flag
  it with annotation_confidence = "Low" and a note, rather than skipping
  it silently.
