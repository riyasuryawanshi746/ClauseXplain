PHASE 2 RESEARCH QUESTIONS -- ClauseXplain
=============================================

REVISED from the Phase 2 prompt's initial draft, based on what Phase 1's
actual data supports. Each RQ below states what it needs to be answerable
and whether that need is currently met.

RQ1 -- Structured risk representation
----------------------------------------
"Can a five-dimension risk annotation schema (unfairness, ambiguity,
non-compliance, precedent-match, enforceability) capture meaningfully
different information than a single overall risk label, for contractual
clauses?"

Needs: inter-annotator agreement data across all five dimensions on a
shared sample (see the agreement design below), plus a check for whether
the five dimensions are highly correlated in practice (if unfairness and
enforceability always move together, the schema may be over-specified).
STATUS: not yet answerable -- no annotations exist yet. This RQ defines
what Phase 2's actual annotation work (beyond this specification) must
produce data for.

RQ2 -- Symbolic rules as a complement to neural representations
--------------------------------------------------------------------
"Does an explicit Indian statutory rule table (indian_legal_rules.csv)
provide risk signal that a neural model trained only on CUAD's category
labels would miss?"

Needs: a working neural baseline (Phase 3+, out of scope here) and a way
to measure whether symbolic-rule flags correlate with, or diverge from,
neural-only risk predictions on the SAME clauses. Given that CUAD provides
zero training signal for 6 of 18 categories (Indemnification,
Confidentiality, Privacy/Data Protection, Modification, Force Majeure,
Payment), this RQ's most informative test case is likely those six
categories specifically, since only the symbolic layer can say anything
about them at all until supplementary data exists.
STATUS: infrastructure (the rule table) exists as of Phase 2; the
comparison itself is Phase 3+ work.

RQ3 -- Does separating Commercial Terms from Payment improve taxonomy
consistency?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
This RQ is answerable NOW, retrospectively, from Phase 1's data, and the
answer is a qualified yes on legal grounds, unverified on statistical
grounds:
  - Legal grounds: Payment (invoicing/timing) and Commercial Terms
    (deal-structure economics) engage different statutory regimes --
    MSMED Act Sections 15-16 apply specifically to payment timing, with
    no comparable statute governing minimum-commitment or revenue-share
    terms. Keeping them separate lets legal-rule citations stay precise
    (see indian_legal_rules.csv: MSMED rules apply only to the Payment
    category).
  - Statistical grounds: NOT yet tested. "Improves taxonomy consistency"
    would ideally be measured via inter-annotator agreement on category
    assignment (do annotators agree more often when the categories are
    split than when they were merged?) -- this requires two independent
    annotation passes, which have not been run. Report the legal
    justification as the current basis for the decision, and flag the
    statistical question as open.

RQ4 -- How much additional Indian-law annotation is required to
supplement CUAD?
---------------------------------------------------------------------
Answerable in part now: exactly 6 of 18 categories (33%) have zero
CUAD-derived spans, and a 7th (Dispute Resolution) has narrow,
single-source coverage (173 spans, only via "Covenant Not To Sue," which
does not address arbitration/forum clauses at all). A defensible target
for a first supplementary annotation pass would be roughly 50-100 clauses
per zero-coverage category from real or synthetic Indian contracts --
enough for meaningful qualitative analysis and rule validation, though
not enough for training a neural classifier from scratch. This number is
a starting estimate, not derived from a power calculation, and should be
revisited once initial annotation reveals how much variation exists
within each category.

ADDITIONAL RQ SUGGESTED BY PHASE 1/2 FINDINGS (not in the original list)

RQ5 -- Does the "Competitive Restriction Exception" polarity problem
generalize?
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Phase 1 found that CUAD's "Competitive Restriction Exception" category is
a risk-REDUCING carve-out incorrectly grouped with risk-increasing
Non-Compete language by simple category mapping. This raises a broader
question: how many other clause categories, across the taxonomy, contain
a similar "exception/carve-out" subtype that a category-level risk model
would mis-score if not explicitly separated? This is answerable by manual
review of the mapping_review_sample.csv / risk_annotation_sample.csv
during annotation, and should be tracked as a specific checklist item
during Phase 2 human review, not assumed to be a one-off issue.

INTER-ANNOTATOR AGREEMENT DESIGN (supports RQ1, RQ3)
---------------------------------------------------------
Proposed protocol, not yet executed:
  - Sample: risk_annotation_sample.csv (target ~200 clauses, see that
    file), or a subset of it if full independent double-annotation of 200
    is too time-consuming initially -- even 50 double-annotated clauses
    would give a preliminary signal.
  - Annotators: minimum 2 independent researcher-annotators (not
    necessarily legal experts, per the project's current resource
    constraints -- see FINAL_DATASET_README.txt). Each annotates the full
    sample independently, without seeing the other's labels.
  - Fields evaluated for agreement:
      - clausexplain_category (already assigned in Phase 1, but worth
        re-checking on the needs_review=True subset specifically)
      - risk_level (discrete: Low/Medium/High) -- use Cohen's kappa
      - legal_risk_type (multi-label) -- use a set-overlap metric (e.g.
        Jaccard similarity per record, averaged) rather than kappa, since
        kappa is designed for single-label agreement
      - the five continuous dimension scores -- use intraclass
        correlation coefficient (ICC), not kappa, since these are
        continuous, not categorical
  - Disagreement handling: any record where the two annotators' risk_level
    differs, or where kappa/ICC for a field falls below a pre-registered
    threshold (e.g. kappa < 0.6, commonly cited as the boundary between
    "moderate" and "substantial" agreement), goes to a third-annotator
    adjudication pass. Record BOTH original annotations plus the
    adjudicated final value -- never silently overwrite.
  - Reporting: report kappa/ICC per field, not a single aggregate number,
    since agreement will likely vary a lot between e.g. category
    assignment (probably high agreement) and unfairness_score (probably
    lower, since it is the most subjective dimension).

STATUS: this is a DESIGN, not a completed process. As of end of Phase 2,
zero clauses have been independently double-annotated. No kappa/ICC value
should appear anywhere in the paper until this process actually runs.
