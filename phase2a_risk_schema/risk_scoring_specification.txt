RISK SCORING SPECIFICATION -- ClauseXplain Phase 2
====================================================

STATUS: This is a PROPOSED SPECIFICATION for future computation. As of the
end of Phase 2, no clause in clauses_risk_annotation.jsonl has an actual
non-null risk_score computed from real, non-null dimension scores. This
document defines the formula so that when annotation (human or validated
rule-based) actually happens, everyone computes the score the same way.

1. FORMULA
----------
Risk = w1*U + w2*A + w3*NC + w4*P + w5*E

Where:
  U  = unfairness_score           (0.0 - 1.0)
  A  = ambiguity_score            (0.0 - 1.0)
  NC = non_compliance_score       (0.0 - 1.0)
  P  = precedent_match_score      (0.0 - 1.0)
  E  = enforceability_score       (0.0 - 1.0)

  w1..w5 are non-negative weights. No specific numeric values are fixed at
  this stage of the project. Treat w1..w5 as UNSET / EXPERIMENTAL until an
  ablation study (varying each weight and measuring effect on agreement
  with human-annotated ground truth, once it exists) is actually run.

  A reasonable placeholder for early experimentation, IF a single set of
  numbers is needed to unblock development (e.g. to build a UI mockup),
  is equal weighting: w1=w2=w3=w4=w5=0.2. This is explicitly a
  PLACEHOLDER, not a research finding, and must be labeled as such
  anywhere it appears (e.g. code comments, UI, paper drafts).

2. COMPONENT DEFINITIONS
-------------------------
unfairness_score:
  Degree to which the clause imposes materially asymmetric obligations,
  rights, or remedies between the parties. Not a legal-validity judgment
  by itself -- Indian contract law does not have a general, freestanding
  "unfairness" doctrine for commercial contracts between parties of
  roughly equal bargaining power (unlike, e.g., consumer-protection
  contexts). Scored based on textual/structural asymmetry, not a legal
  conclusion.

ambiguity_score:
  Degree to which the clause's operative language is vague, uses
  undefined terms, or is reasonably open to more than one interpretation
  that would produce materially different outcomes.

non_compliance_score:
  Degree to which the clause appears to conflict with a SPECIFIC,
  identified Indian statutory provision. Must trace to an actual row in
  indian_legal_rules.csv via the legal_provision field -- a
  non_compliance_score set without a corresponding legal_provision
  citation is invalid and should be treated as a data-quality error.

precedent_match_score:
  Similarity to a previously identified risky clause pattern. AS OF END
  OF PHASE 2, ClauseXplain has no curated precedent/case-law corpus.
  This field is null for every record and cannot be legitimately
  populated until such a corpus is built (Phase 3+ work). Do not
  approximate this with a proxy metric (e.g. embedding similarity to
  other CUAD spans) without clearly documenting the proxy as such.

enforceability_score:
  Degree of concern that the clause, in whole or part, may be
  unenforceable under Indian law (e.g. an overbroad non-compete). This
  overlaps conceptually with non_compliance_score but is scored
  separately because a clause can be technically non-compliant with a
  provision's literal text while remaining likely enforceable in
  practice (courts often sever an offending portion rather than voiding
  an entire clause) -- and vice versa.

3. NORMALIZATION
-----------------
Each component score: 0.0 <= score <= 1.0
0.0 = no concern along this dimension
1.0 = maximum concern along this dimension
Scores are continuous, not restricted to discrete steps, though initial
human annotation guidelines (see PHASE2_ANNOTATION_GUIDELINES.txt) may
ask annotators to anchor on Low/Medium/High bands that map to
approximate ranges, for consistency.

4. INITIAL OPERATIONAL THRESHOLDS (risk_level from risk_score)
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Low:    risk_score in [0.00, 0.33]
Medium: risk_score in (0.33, 0.66]
High:   risk_score in (0.66, 1.00]

These are ClauseXplain's PROPOSED OPERATIONAL THRESHOLDS ONLY. They are
NOT derived from any Indian statute or judicial standard -- no Indian law
defines contractual risk as falling into three numeric bands. They exist
purely so the system can produce a discrete, user-facing label from a
continuous score. Before relying on these thresholds for any evaluation
claim, run a sensitivity analysis: does classification accuracy/agreement
change meaningfully if the boundaries move to, e.g., 0.30/0.60 or
0.40/0.70? Report the thresholds actually used in any published result,
and treat the specific values 0.33/0.66 as a starting assumption to be
tested, not a finding.

5. WHAT THIS SPECIFICATION DOES NOT DO
------------------------------------------
- It does not assign actual weight values (w1..w5) -- future work.
- It does not compute risk_score for any real clause -- future work,
  contingent on actual non-null dimension scores existing first.
- It does not validate the formula's structure (e.g. whether a linear
  weighted sum is even the right functional form, vs. e.g. a maximum-
  of-components approach for a "any single severe dimension dominates"
  model) -- this should be tested empirically once annotated data exists,
  not assumed.
