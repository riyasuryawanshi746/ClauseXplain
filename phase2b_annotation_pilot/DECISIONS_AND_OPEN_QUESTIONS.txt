DECISIONS AND OPEN QUESTIONS -- Phase 2B build
=================================================
Things discovered while inspecting Phase 2A output and building the
annotation app. Items 1-3 were flagged as open in the original Phase 2B
build and have since been RESOLVED (see each item below); item 4 remains
accurate as a running record of what was and wasn't done automatically.

1. DISCRETE VS CONTINUOUS SCORING SCALE -- RESOLVED
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Decision: standardized on the discrete 5-point scale
(0.00/0.25/0.50/0.75/1.00), matching what the app and validator already
enforced. PHASE2_ANNOTATION_GUIDELINES.txt and risk_scoring_specification.txt
were both updated to describe the discrete scale explicitly (their prior
"continuous 0.0-1.0" language is gone, replaced with the five named
bands). phase2_annotation_schema.json (now version 2.1) adds
allowed_values listing exactly these five values plus null for every
score field. All three documents plus the app and validator are now
consistent.

2. PROVISIONAL RISK SCORE: 4-DIMENSION AVERAGE VS "PENDING" -- RESOLVED
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
Decision confirmed and formalized: provisional_risk_score_4dim and
provisional_risk_level_4dim are now formally defined in
phase2_annotation_schema.json (version 2.1) and given their own section
(section 4) in risk_scoring_specification.txt, which explicitly
distinguishes them from the canonical 5-dimension final_risk_score /
risk_level and states why equal-weight averaging over 4 dimensions was
chosen over treating the missing precedent score as 0. Canonical
risk_score/risk_level remain null on every record, enforced by
validate_phase2_annotations.py.

2b. INDIAN LEGAL RULE TYPES -- RESOLVED (new item, not in original list)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
indian_legal_rules.csv now has a rule_type column (risk_trigger /
contextual / gap_detection) for all 15 rules, reviewed individually
rather than defaulted. 7 risk_trigger, 4 contextual, 4 gap_detection.
The Force Majeure rule (ICA_S56_01) was classified gap_detection rather
than a generic "contextual," since it specifically concerns the ABSENCE
of a force majeure clause, matching gap_detection's definition more
precisely than a general contextual note would.

3. SAMPLE REBUILD (see SAMPLE_METHODOLOGY_NOTE.txt)
-----------------------------------------------------
Decision: keep the existing 200-clause sample as-is. Category
stratification is solid (12/18 categories, 16-18 each); the length-band
skew toward long contracts likely reflects the underlying CUAD corpus and
is recorded as a known limitation rather than fixed by resampling. Full
reasoning in SAMPLE_METHODOLOGY_NOTE.txt.

3b. PILOT SELECTION -- IMPROVED (new item, not in original list)
-----------------------------------------------------------------
The original pilot logic used the sample's first 10 rows, which covered
only 6 of 12 populated categories (checked directly, not assumed).
generate_pilot_set.py now builds a deterministic pilot maximizing
category variety (10 of 12 categories, one clause per category in
alphabetical order), written to pilot_clause_ids.json.
annotation_app.py, generate_pilot_review.py both now read this file.

4. WHAT I DID NOT DO
------------------------
- I did not annotate any clauses myself. Every score, note, and legal
  citation in risk_annotations_human.jsonl must come from you running
  annotation_app.py -- I have no basis (and no license under this
  project's own rules) to guess what a specific clause's unfairness or
  ambiguity score should be.
- I did not write pilot_annotation_review.txt's qualitative section --
  generate_pilot_review.py produces the quantitative summary from your
  real pilot data and leaves the qualitative questions (Step 17) for you
  to answer after you've actually used the interface.
- I did not fix numeric weights (w1..w5) for the eventual 5-dimension
  risk formula -- risk_scoring_specification.txt is explicit that this
  requires an ablation study against real annotated data, which doesn't
  exist yet.


5. PROVENANCE MODEL EXTENDED FOR AI DRAFTS -- RESOLVED (schema v2.1 -> v2.2)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Problem found when the researcher asked for an AI-assisted pilot: the v2.1
annotation_source value set was closed and contained no honest option for
model-generated labels. The two near-fits both falsify provenance --
"human_researcher_annotation" asserts a human entered judgments they did
not, and "rule_based_preannotation" asserts deterministic output of
indian_legal_rules.csv from a "rule_engine_v1" that does not exist. Writing
outside the set fails validation; writing inside it corrupts the one field
a reviewer must be able to trust, and would silently contaminate any later
kappa calculation by making human-vs-model agreement look human-vs-human.

Resolution (authorized by the researcher before any annotation was written):
  - annotation_source += "ai_assisted_preannotation"
  - new required boolean field reviewed_by_human (default False)
  - AI drafts use a model annotator_id, never a human one
  - AI drafts are written ONLY to risk_annotations_ai_pilot.jsonl;
    risk_annotations_human.jsonl remains reserved for human work
  - validate_phase2_annotations.py now enforces all of the above, including
    rejecting a human-looking annotator_id on an AI record and requiring
    reviewed_by_human=True on human_researcher_annotation records.

An AI draft is promoted to human data only by the researcher reviewing it,
setting reviewed_by_human=True and changing annotation_source. Until then it
is explicitly not research data.
