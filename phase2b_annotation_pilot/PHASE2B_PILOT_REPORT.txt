C. ANNOTATION METHODOLOGY
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
Scale: discrete 5-point {0.00, 0.25, 0.50, 0.75, 1.00}.
Dimensions scored: unfairness, ambiguity, non_compliance, enforceability.
Precedent: precedent_match_score = null on every record. No precedent
  corpus exists. Null was NOT replaced with 0.
Canonical score: risk_score = null, risk_level = null on every record,
  because the 5-dimension formula cannot be computed without precedent.
Provisional: provisional_risk_score_4dim = (U+A+NC+E)/4, exactly per
  risk_scoring_specification.txt s.4b. Banded with the same 0.33/0.66
  thresholds. No new weights were invented.
Legal rules: only indian_legal_rules.csv was used. Rule types
  (risk_trigger / contextual / gap_detection) were respected — no
  contextual rule was treated as a violation.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------
E. JUSTIFICATION OF SIGNIFICANT DECISIONS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
Clause 1 SKIPPED. The whole span is 38 characters ending in a colon:
  "Licensor may terminate this Agreement:". The operative conditions are
  absent. Scoring anything — including 0.00 — would invent certainty from
  missing text. Skipped with a recorded reason, per the guidelines.

Clause 8 is the only Medium. ICA_S27_02's trigger has two limbs (broad
  scope OR indefinite duration). Duration here is definite (2 years) and
  does not trigger; scope does, because limb (ii) forbids interference
  "in any manner". Applied on the scope limb only, hence non_compliance
  0.25 rather than 0.50. Unfairness held at 0.25 because the clause is
  expressly mutual. Flagged for review, not asserted as unenforceable.

Clause 10 deliberately did NOT apply S.74. CUAD calls it "Liquidated
  Damages" and the mapping sends it to "Penalties", but the payment
  trigger is Dova's own election to terminate, and the payment is
  consideration for services already performed — not a sum payable on
  breach. Applying s.74 would have been precisely the error the
  methodology warns against.

Clause 7 confirmed the polarity problem empirically and extended it: the
  span is not a pure carve-out as Phase 1 assumed, but restriction AND
  carve-out in one span.

Clause 4 scored 0.00 across all four dimensions despite being a Delaware
  governing-law clause — the single clause most likely to matter to an
  Indian SME. The rule inventory contains nothing on choice of law, and
  importing outside legal knowledge was prohibited. The concern is
  recorded as a rule gap instead.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------
G. LEGAL-RULE GAPS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
GAP 1 (most important). No rule covers CHOICE OF LAW or FORUM. Clause 4
  is a foreign (Delaware) governing-law clause and the inventory produced
  nothing. For a tool aimed at Indian freelancers and SMEs, "this contract
  is governed by foreign law and disputes are heard abroad" is arguably
  the single most actionable flag the system could raise. Candidate for a
  new rule — likely contextual or gap_detection, not risk_trigger.
GAP 2. Liability's only rule (ICA_S73_01, contextual) concerns damages
  where the contract is silent. It does not reach insurance, additional-
  insured status, or limitation-of-liability drafting (clause 6).
GAP 3. "Other" is served only by ICA_S23_01, a general validity backstop.
  Audit rights (clause 9) — a real commercial protection — had no rule.
GAP 4. Nothing covers cost/expense-shifting mechanisms.
GAP 5. Indemnification has a rule (ICA_S124_01), but its trigger requires
  "uncapped or unusually broad". A well-drafted, carved-out indemnity like
  clause 3 falls through entirely — the inventory cannot say "this is a
  competently drafted indemnity", only "this one is alarming".

POTENTIAL FUTURE LEGAL-CONTEXT NOTES (recorded separately, NOT used in
any annotation, per the pilot brief):
  - Foreign governing law / foreign seat raises practical access-to-
    remedy issues for Indian SMEs that no current rule expresses.
  - Post-termination employee non-solicitation is treated differently in
    Indian practice from a restraint on a party's own trade; the
    inventory's s.27 rules do not draw that distinction sharply.
  These are flagged for the researcher to evaluate — they are NOT
  incorporated into any score or citation above.

H. GUIDELINE ISSUES DISCOVERED
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
ISSUE 1 — CATEGORY vs SUBSTANCE CONFLICT
  Why it matters: determines which rules are even searched.
  Evidence: clause 3 (Dispute Resolution label, indemnity substance);
    clause 10 (Penalties label, compensatory substance).
  Proposed correction: instruct annotators to read the text first,
    independently of its category label, and add a field to record a
    suspected category mismatch.
  Guidelines change? YES.  Schema change? YES (new optional field).

ISSUE 2 — "ZERO" AND "CANNOT ASSESS" ARE INDISTINGUISHABLE
  Why it matters: clause 4 scored 0.0000 provisional. A downstream reader
    cannot tell that from a genuinely assessed low-risk clause. The
    guidelines say null means "not yet assessed", but the validator
    requires non-null scores on completed records — so "cannot assess"
    has nowhere to go except an inappropriate 0.00 or a skip.
  Evidence: clauses 4 and 9.
  Proposed correction: add a per-dimension "assessable" flag, or permit
    null scores on completed records with a mandatory reason.
  Guidelines change? YES.  Schema change? YES.

ISSUE 3 — NO INSTRUCTION FOR PARTIAL TRIGGER SATISFACTION
  Why it matters: rules with multi-limb triggers are common; without
    guidance, two annotators will score the same clause differently.
  Evidence: clause 8 (scope limb met, duration limb not).
  Proposed correction: state explicitly how to score partial matches.
  Guidelines change? YES.  Schema change? NO.

ISSUE 4 — requires_legal_review IS ALWAYS TRUE, SO IT CARRIES NO SIGNAL
  Why it matters: a field with one possible value cannot prioritise
    anything. The intended purpose (flagging clauses needing expert
    attention) is defeated.
  Evidence: clause 5 is a plain non-transferable licence grant. Setting
    False was REJECTED by validation during this pilot and corrected to
    True in deference to the guidelines.
  Proposed correction: either rename to reflect its true meaning (e.g.
    "not_legally_validated", always True), or allow False and introduce a
    separate always-True disclaimer field.
  Guidelines change? YES.  Schema change? PROBABLY.

ISSUE 5 — indian_law_relevant HAS NO "UNCERTAIN" VALUE
  Why it matters: the app offers Yes/No/Uncertain but the schema type is
    boolean|null, and null fails the required-field check on completed
    records. "Uncertain" is unrepresentable.
  Evidence: clauses 7 and 10 are genuinely uncertain; both were forced to
    a boolean with the uncertainty pushed into free text.
  Guidelines change? NO.  Schema change? YES.

ISSUE 6 — NO WAY TO RECORD MIXED POLARITY
  Evidence: clause 7 restricts and carves out in one span.
  Guidelines change? YES.  Schema change? POSSIBLY.

NO CHANGE WAS MADE TO THE GUIDELINES OR THE RISK METHODOLOGY DURING THIS
PILOT. The only schema change was the authorised v2.1 -> v2.2 provenance
fix. Issues 1-6 are recorded for the researcher's decision, not applied.

I. DATASET LIMITATIONS OBSERVED
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
SPAN INCOMPLETENESS: 1 of 10 spans (clause 1, 10%) was too fragmentary to
  annotate at all. If that rate holds across 200, roughly 20 clauses are
  unusable — material for the paper's limitations section.
REDACTION: 3 of 10 spans (clauses 2, 9, 10) have '[***]' / '[*]'
  redactions removing the operative number, and in clause 9 even the
  identity of the paying party. SEC-filed contracts are redacted as a
  matter of course; this is systemic, not incidental. It directly blocks
  proportionality assessment, which is central to unfairness scoring.
CROSS-REFERENCES: 6 of 10 spans reference sections or defined terms
  outside the span ("Section 6.1.1", "Tail Period", "Owner Indemnified
  Parties", "Section 12.3.1"). The span-level unit of analysis is in
  tension with clause-level legal reasoning.
CATEGORY RELIABILITY: 2 of 10 (clauses 3, 10) appear substantively
  mismatched to their assigned category, and clause 1's category cannot
  be verified from its text. Phase 1's needs_review flags were warranted.
JURISDICTION: clause 4 is Delaware-governed — a live demonstration of the
  CUAD jurisdiction mismatch already documented in Phase 1.
NOTABLE: Indemnification is listed as one of the six zero-CUAD-coverage
  categories, yet clause 3 IS an indemnity clause that arrived through
  CUAD "Covenant Not To Sue". The zero-coverage finding may understate
  actual coverage — some target categories may exist inside CUAD spans
  filed under other labels. Worth checking before treating the six as
  absolutely absent.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------
L. HUMAN REVIEW REQUIREMENT
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
These 10 records are drafts. They become research data only when the
researcher reviews each one, corrects what is wrong, sets
reviewed_by_human=true and changes annotation_source to
"human_researcher_annotation". Until then they must not be counted as
annotated data, cited as results, or used as a baseline for agreement.

A critical caveat: because these drafts exist, there is now an anchoring
risk. A reviewer who reads a draft first will tend to agree with it. The
pilot's actual purpose is to test whether a HUMAN finds the guidelines
usable — and a model finding them usable says little about that. Reading
each clause independently before reading the draft would preserve more of
the pilot's diagnostic value.
